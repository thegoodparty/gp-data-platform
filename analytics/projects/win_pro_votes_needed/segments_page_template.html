The answer

If texts are bundled, size the allowance by race size, not by customer segment: race size is what changes how many texts a customer sends. Customers CaS reached did outreach far more often than any segment differs, but we can't yet tell whether CaS causes it.

Whether CaS reached them goes with it far more than any segment does. CaS reached {{PRE_N}} of {{N}} customers before they started any outreach, and {{OUT_PRE}} of them did outreach, {{CAS_PRE_ABOVE}} points above everyone ({{OUT_ALL}}). {{OUT_HERO}}

Those CaS reached may also send more texts. Among activated customers who texted, the median was {{ACT_TX_ENG}} texts with CaS contact against {{ACT_TX_NO}} without. But the group without is {{ACT_TX_NO_N}} people, and their median could be anywhere from {{ACT_TX_CI}} of the others' (95% range), so this could be noise.

{{TX_HERO}}

For pricing: segments are not a basis for tiers. Support may be the bigger lever, but test it before pricing it.

Limits

What we don't know

  • Whether CaS causes the difference. Customers choose to take the call, so some would have done outreach, and sent more texts, anyway. The order fits: CaS contact came before the first text for {{CAS_FIRST}} texters with both.
  • Smaller segment effects, and small groups. Differences in texts under about {{POWER_X}} times would not show here, and groups like federal races are too small to check across signup eras.
  • Exact text counts for some customers. That is why the share over 5,000 is a range.
Recommendations

What should product do with this?

The analyst's read, revised after the methodology and product reviews.

  1. If texts are bundled, scale the allowance with votes needed to win. A single 5,000-text allowance is too small for most texters in races needing 2,500 votes or more. Section 1
  2. Don't build tiers on customer segments. No segment we could check changes how much customers send, and none moves whether they do outreach by more than {{SEG_GAP_MAX}} points. Section 2
  3. Test support as a packaging lever before pricing it. Customers CaS reached did more outreach, but they chose to take the call. A randomized change to the upgrade call, such as booking it for half of new upgrades, would show whether CaS causes it. Section 2

Terms. CaS: the candidate success team. Every upgrade is offered a CaS call; reached by CaS means at least one call or meeting, or two emails or texts, by election day. Self-serve: no CaS contact. Texter: sent at least one text in the six months up to election day.

A2 {{SEP}} Volume

Does CaS or race size change how much people text and spend?

Race size does, clearly. CaS possibly does for texts, not for spend. Texters with no CaS contact sent a median of {{MED_TX_NO}} texts against {{MED_TX_ENG}} for those with it, and race size does not explain the gap: at 2,500 votes or more it is {{BIG_TX_NO}} against {{BIG_TX_ENG}}. It rests on {{NOCAS_TX_K}} self-serve texters with a known count, and their median is {{NOCAS_TX_CI}} of everyone else's at 95% confidence, so no difference is still possible. Median outreach spend per payer is {{MED_USD_NO}} and {{MED_USD_ENG}}, within noise. By race size the median texter goes from {{BAND_LO}} texts under 1,000 votes to {{BAND_HI}} at 10,000+. A 5,000-text allowance covers at least half of texters in small races but not most in bigger ones: {{O5_SMALL}}, of texters under 1,000 votes went past it, against {{O5_BIG}}, at 2,500 votes or more.

A3 {{SEP}} ICP and CaS

Why does ICP office look like a segment?

ICP is our own label, and CaS works ICP customers harder, so this cannot say whether ICP is a customer type. What it shows: customers CaS never reached text at the same rate whatever their office ({{ICP_NO}}). The ICP gap appears only among engaged customers ({{ICP_PRE}} when CaS came first), so ICP is not a self-serve customer type. CaS help seems to turn into outreach more readily for ICP races, which fits Win supporting those offices best.

Appendix

What was asked, and how we framed it

The question as asked

If product is looking for ways to differentiate Win Pro users to inform pricing strategy, which cuts actually separate them? We tested one hypothesis in DATA-2809, race size (votes needed to win), and mostly answered it. I can't make recommendations to product from one cut. I want to know which dimensions produce meaningfully different usage, in text volume, outreach spend, channel mix, or the features people use, so pricing could be built on them (tiers, bundles, add-ons). "Nothing separates them beyond race size" is a valid answer.
